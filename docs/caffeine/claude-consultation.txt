## Model: what is known vs. what is illustrative

**Known mechanisms (can be stated as fact, with sources):**
- Oral absorption is almost complete (F ≈ 1), with a peak at roughly 30–60 min (range about 15–120).
- About 95% is cleared by the liver enzyme CYP1A2. The main product is paraxanthine, which is also active.
- In healthy adults the elimination half-life is usually about 3–7 h, wider across individuals.
- Caffeine works as a competitive antagonist at adenosine A1 and A2A receptors. Adenosine still builds up during waking; caffeine hides its signal and does not remove it or pay back sleep debt.
- Effects depend on dose. Tolerance is real. In regular users, part of the felt benefit is relief of overnight withdrawal.
- Caffeine taken close to bedtime can lengthen time to fall asleep and reduce deep sleep. Sensitivity varies.

**Illustrative parameters (label them that way):**
- Absorption rate ka ≈ 4/h, giving a peak at about 50 min.
- Half-life 5 h, with a band from the 3 h and 7 h curves.
- The alerting index uses a saturating curve, E = C/(C+EC50), with EC50 picked for teaching purposes.
- Sleep pressure is a simple "Process S" curve: it rises toward a ceiling while awake and falls during sleep.
- Felt sleepiness = S·(1 − k·E). This keeps it at or below S and returns it to S as caffeine clears, with no invented "crash".

**Plot units:** plot **mg remaining in the body** from the one-compartment absorption/elimination formula (Bateman). Do not plot a plasma concentration, since that needs a volume of distribution and adds a second assumption.

**Variability to mention as text only:** pregnancy (much longer half-life), oral contraceptives (about doubles it), smoking (shortens it), drugs that inhibit CYP1A2 such as fluvoxamine, liver disease, genetics, and how much caffeine is actually in a cup (roughly 60–200 mg).

**Do not change:** glucose/insulin, glycogen, heart rate, fuel mix, recovery, energy expenditure, sleep timing or sleep quality.

## Tests to add

- **Caffeine curve:**
  - Zero before drinking.
  - Mass balance: absorbed + remaining + eliminated = dose, within 1e-6.
  - Peak at 30–90 min.
  - After absorption, the ratio over one half-life ≈ 0.5.
  - Band ordered low ≤ mid ≤ high.
  - Doubling the dose doubles the curve; values never negative.
  - A later time leaves more caffeine at sleep start (09:30 vs 16:00).
- **Isolation (most important):**
  - `simulateDay` states, ledger and budget are identical with coffee on/off and at any dose or time.
  - The long-term and sprint outputs don't change.
- **Effect model:**
  - Sleep pressure is identical with and without coffee.
  - Felt ≤ pressure at all times.
  - Felt returns to within ε of pressure as caffeine → 0, with no overshoot.
  - E rises with concentration and stays below 1.
- **Narration:**
  - Coffee cues exist only when coffee is on, and none fall in sleep or exercise.
  - Existing cues are not cut below ~8 min.
  - A text lint forbids "removes/clears adenosine", "repay", "fat", "metabolism boost", "dopamine", "cortisol" and "heart rate rises".
- **Browser:**
  - Controls change the plots without moving the scrubber, and work with keyboard and on mobile (followed plot).
  - Reduced motion shows the static cup.
  - Export round-trips the setting.
  - Assert that the "Inside this day" and "Guided" elements are absent.

## Scientific cautions for the UI text

- Present this as a teaching illustration for one healthy adult on one day, not a dose recommendation. Don't call any amount "safe"; just cite EFSA (about 200 mg single dose / 400 mg per day for healthy adults, 200 mg/day in pregnancy) as a reference.
- Caffeine in tea, cola or chocolate on the diet menu is not counted; only this coffee event is.
