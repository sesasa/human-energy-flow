## `src/visual/plates.ts` (about 205 lines; no other files touched)

**What it exports:** `mealPlate(meal: Meal, idPrefix?: string): string`, which imports `foodById` and `Meal` from `../model/diet`. It returns one SVG with `viewBox="0 0 320 240"`, `role="img"`, and an `aria-label` plus `<title>` listing the actual foods and grams, e.g. `Eggs on toast: Eggs 100 g (2 eggs), Wholemeal bread 120 g (3 slices), …`.

**How a plate is built**
1. **Inputs:** portions with 0 g, invalid or unknown IDs are skipped. If the same food appears twice, its grams are added together.
2. **Sorting the food:**
   - **Sides:** milk is drawn as a glass and yogurt as a small ceramic bowl, both to the right of the plate. The plate then moves left and shrinks slightly.
   - **Garnish:** oil is a thin gold drizzle with droplets, and salt is a few white grains. Both sit on top of the food.
   - **Bowl:** if there are oats, the plate becomes a deep bowl filled with porridge, and the fruit and nuts sit on top.
   - **Plate items:** main foods (meat, fish, eggs, starches, bread, cake) come first, then small accompaniments. Each group is ordered by its share of a typical portion. At most 7 are drawn; any extras still appear in the label.
3. **Layout:** each item count from 1 to 7 has a fixed set of positions, biggest first, so the plate doesn't get cluttered.
4. **Size:** drawn size is `slot × clamp(0.82·√(grams/typical), 0.45, 1.08)`, so area grows with weight but always fits its slot. Eggs (50 g each) and bread (40 g per slice) are drawn as separate pieces, up to 4, with a small `×N` mark when there are more.
5. **Drawings:** native SVG paths with gradients, highlights and soft shadows on a dark sage background and a cream ceramic plate. Every food type in the catalogue has its own drawing, for example:
   - grill-marked chicken slices
   - steak with a fat rim
   - salmon with fat lines, or striped mackerel skin
   - halved eggs with yolks, and bread slices with crust and seeds
   - rice grains (white or brown), wedge-cut potatoes, a spaghetti nest
   - broccoli florets, spinach leaves, carrot rounds, tomato slices
   - fruit, almonds or walnuts, cheddar cubes, chocolate squares
   - an iced carrot-cake slice
   
   There's also a simple fallback mound for unknown types. No emoji or external assets are used.
6. **IDs:** gradient IDs are `${prefix}-${name}`. The prefix is cleaned up and defaults to an incrementing counter (`meal-plate-N`), so several plates on one page won't clash.
7. **Repeatable output:** grain and nut scatter uses a seeded random generator, so a given meal always draws the same way.
