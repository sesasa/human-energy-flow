# Profile and cycle evidence

Verified October 2026. These sources support directions and explicit equation choices; they do not validate a personal ferritin or body-composition forecast.

- [Mifflin–St Jeor original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/): resting expenditure estimated from weight, height, age and sex. The implementation uses its common rounded10/6.25/−5 coefficients and male+5/female−161 intercepts.
- [2025 meta-analysis of sex differences in resistance-training hypertrophy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40028215/): similar relative muscle-size gains across sexes; absolute gains can differ. Supports using actual starting tissue rather than a blanket female penalty. This app's gain ceiling and time course remain illustrative.
- [National Academies iron DRI chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK222309/): basal loss around 0.014 mg/kg/day; menstrual cohort medians20–30 mL, modeled median27.6 mL; iron content3.39 mg/g Hb. Blood-loss distributions are broad, and80 mL is a traditional high-loss boundary.
- [Nordic Nutrition Recommendations iron evidence review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870973/): historical cohort median30 mL, mean43.4 mL and90th percentile84 mL illustrate skewed losses. The application's15/30/80 scenarios and their sensitivity ranges are authored; they are not inferred from the user's category choice.
- [Nadler et al.,1962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21936146/), [original publication](https://www.surgjournal.com/article/0039-6060%2862%2990166-6/pdf): sex-specific height/weight blood-volume estimates. Coefficients also reproduced in the methods of [this primary clinical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10025578/). Used only to initialize functional red-cell iron with declared normal-Hb assumptions.
- [Mayo ferritin test catalogue](https://www.mayocliniclabs.com/api/sitecore/testcatalog/TestCatalogSetupPDF?testid=619953): adult male 31–409 µg/L, female 18–50years6–175, female≥51years11–328. User-supplied lab intervals take priority. These defaults do not establish absence/presence or cause of deficiency.
- NIH ODS intake reference tables: [iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/), [calcium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/), [magnesium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/), [potassium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-HealthProfessional/), [zinc](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/), [vitamin C](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/), [vitamin D](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/), [omega-3 ALA](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/). Nonpregnant/nonlactating adult18–85 age groups are supported. Menstrual needs are already represented in the age/sex iron RDA; no extra cycle surcharge is applied to it.

Existing [laboratory sources](../lab-results/SOURCES.md) still cover the rough ferritin/storage relationship and conservative B12/folate/D food-response scenarios. [CoFID provenance](../../data/README.md) is unchanged. No new food nutrient values or random coefficients were introduced. Female preset fractions, schematic anatomy, the within-period taper, regular-cycle bounds and body-composition scope limits are expressly authored teaching choices, not sourced measurements or diagnostic cutoffs.
