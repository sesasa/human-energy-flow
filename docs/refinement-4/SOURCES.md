# Source register for the expanded micronutrient module

Reviewed 2026-10-06. The sources support physiological relationships and marker limitations. The implementation's exact pool anchors, coefficients and trajectories are authored educational assumptions, not validated clinical predictions. Previous iron/B12 sources remain in [the earlier register](../status/SOURCES.md).

- [NIH ODS — Iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/): ferritin as a storage marker, effects of inflammation, heme/non-heme absorption and clinical context. A ferritin observation never directly initializes the simulated iron reserve.
- [NIH ODS — Vitamin B12](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/): substantial stores, limited absorption, and limitations of interpreting serum B12 alone. No B12 lab cutoff or dose recommendation is encoded.
- [NIH ODS — Vitamin D](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/): 25-hydroxyvitamin D is the relevant status marker; sunlight-derived inputs matter; circulating 25-OH D has a roughly 15-day half-life; intake–lab relationships and assays vary. The app's **35-day effective availability-pool buffering** also abstracts tissue storage; it is deliberately not a 25-OH D concentration model or a serum half-life claim. Non-food input is an explicit scenario assumption, never a UV exposure or supplement recommendation.
- [NIH ODS — Folate](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/): estimated body content 15–30 mg; naturally occurring food folate is approximately half bioavailable; serum folate is sensitive to recent intake and red-cell folate is a distinct marker. The existing food catalog is treated as natural food folate. Folic acid supplements/fortified-food DFE kinetics are not reconstructed.
- [NIH ODS — Calcium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/): serum calcium is tightly controlled and does not represent nutritional status or bone reserves. Total and ionized calcium are different tests. This app accepts total serum calcium observations only and makes no reservoir prediction from them.
- [NIH ODS — Magnesium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/): less than 1% is in serum; serum values correlate poorly with total tissue stores. Lab observations and dietary intake are displayed separately, without a generic magnesium tank.
- [NIH ODS — Zinc](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/): serum/plasma values are influenced by sampling conditions and infection and do not directly reflect intake. Zinc is placed under Additional tests, not presented as a routine panel recommended for all users.
- [UK CoFID 2021](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/composition-of-foods-integrated-dataset-cofid): existing per-food quantities drive actual diet changes; see [catalog provenance](../../data/README.md). No food is assigned a new fabricated nutrient profile.

Lab comparisons use only the user's supplied limit(s) in the selected unit. They do not use universal deficiency cutoffs. Unit changes clear the numerical fields rather than silently reinterpreting them. A value outside a supplied range is an observation, not a diagnosis or a percentage of body stores.
