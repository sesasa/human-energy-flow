## Must-fix

1. **The viewer browser suite fails, so later checks never ran.** `artifacts/refinement-4/viewer-browser.txt` shows it failing at `tests/viewer-browser.ts:24`: `seek(1439.9)` on `#scrubber`, which has `step="1"` in day mode (`src/main.ts:87`). Playwright rejects the value as "Malformed value".
   - Not verified as a result: chart click, drag and keyboard; shared playback; return position; the representative weekday menu; the one-off meal not repeating; mobile overflow.
   - The `viewer-long`, `viewer-representative`, `viewer-mobile` and `viewer-mobile-chart` screenshots are taken after line 24, so they must come from an earlier run. They are stale evidence.
   - Fix: use `seek(1439)`, then re-run the suite and regenerate the screenshots.

2. **The trend label always says "slowly", even when the change is fast or barely there.** `storeTrend` (`src/model/micronutrients.ts:121-123`) only produces "Rising slowly", "Falling slowly" or "Nearly steady", based on the first 28 days.
   - Folate with reduced uptake has about a 45-day half-time and falls quickly toward roughly 30/100, yet is labelled "Falling slowly".
   - The headline also only reflects the first month. In `vitamin-d-food-only.png` it says "Rising slowly", but the current diet goes from 45 to 47/100 over a whole year, which is essentially flat.
   - Fix: measure the trend over a longer window (or start vs 12 months) and scale the wording ("Falling", "Falling steadily", …).

3. **Choosing "No known low status" hides the food experiments and the clinical note.** At `src/visual/nutrientPanel.ts:64` both are hidden whenever intent is `none`.
   - Changing an assumption (reduced absorption, or vitamin D food-only) in this mode does not switch the intent. Setting a low starting reserve does switch it (line 48), so the behaviour is inconsistent.
   - In that state there is no clinical note and no food comparison at all.
   - Clicking "No known low status" also silently resets every assumption (line 36).
   - Fix: switch to Hypothetical on any non-default assumption, or always show the food section.

4. **An invalid lab entry leaves the old reading on screen.** When the input has an error, the observation is not updated (`nutrientPanel.ts:46`). The reading line (line 98) keeps showing the previous valid value and its range comparison, right next to the error message. The reading should say "Check the value" while an error is present.

5. **The micronutrient chart labels are inconsistently sized and unreadable on mobile.** The chart's text scales with the chart width (`nutrientPanel.ts:22`).
   - On desktop the axis labels are about 15px while the surrounding text is about 8px (`micro-baseline.png`).
   - On a 390px screen the labels shrink to roughly 5px (`micro-mobile-trend.png`).
   - Many captions ("First-week daily means", "Below model reference") are about 8px on every screen size, which is too small for a general audience.

## What meets the brief (from the code)

- **Labs:**
  - `simulateNutrients` never reads lab observations.
  - Comparisons use only the limits the user supplies.
  - Changing the unit clears the numbers.
  - Calcium, magnesium and zinc are observation and intake only.
  - The NIH notes are cautious.
- **Distinct time scales:**
  - Vitamin D (35-day buffering), folate (1.55% a day), B12 (0.1% a day) and iron are clearly different.
  - The vitamin D background input of 6.3 µg/day is visible and can be removed.
  - The vitamin D pool is explicitly not a serum half-life or a forecast of a 25-OH D result.
- **Diet comparison:**
  - The applied-diet baseline is compared against a separate draft that shares the same starting assumptions.
  - Food suggestions use real portions and keep calories about level.
  - Repeat clicks don't stack.
  - Macros, absorption and pools are recalculated.
- **Viewer timeline:**
  - The chart and playback share one timeline position.
  - The exact long-term position is remembered for the return.
  - The representative day says it uses the starting body and is not a reconstructed history.
  - A one-off day is excluded from week 2 onward through the settled-week simulation.

## Assessment for a layperson

The day viewer is clear and illustrative: the seated or eating figure with its caption, the anatomy placed higher, and two-column measures that change with the scene all work well. The micronutrient flow (starting point, lab result, model reserve, current diet, food experiment) is logical and careful.

The main weaknesses are a lot of very small caveat text and trend wording that can mislead (#2). I would not call it finished until #1 is fixed and re-run, and #2–#5 are addressed. After that it is a strong, honest educational tool.
