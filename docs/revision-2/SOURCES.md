# Revision 2 source register

Reviewed for relationships and anatomical placement, not used to claim a clinically calibrated simulation. Existing nutrition, day-energy and adaptation references remain in [the original register](../SOURCES.md).

| Source | Supports | Limits on application |
|---|---|---|
| [Gaitanos et al., 1993 — Human muscle metabolism during intermittent maximal exercise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8226473/) | Ten 6-second maximal cycling efforts with 30-second recovery; rapid PCr depletion, substantial initial glycolysis, declining glycolytic contribution and reduced power later. | Eight men; cycling, ten efforts. Does not validate our 20-repetition running trajectory, fatigue index or exact power curve. |
| [Girard et al., 2011 — Repeated-sprint ability, factors contributing to fatigue](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21780851/) | Fatigue has interacting metabolic and neuromuscular contributors. | Review, not a numerical prescription for the implemented coefficients. |
| [Bogdanis, 2012 — Effects of physical activity and inactivity on muscle fatigue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3355468/) | Context for PCr availability, oxygen-dependent recovery and changing energy-system contributions. | Different study protocols and participant fitness cannot be collapsed into a universal curve. |
| [Dawson et al., 1997 — Muscle phosphocreatine repletion following single and repeated short sprint efforts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9241025/) | PCr resynthesis during recovery after short efforts. | Actual kinetics vary; the model's base time constant and walking multiplier are illustrative. |
| [Mendez-Villanueva et al., 2012 — Recovery of repeated-sprint exercise is associated with PCr resynthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23284836/) | PCr restoration relates to recovered performance, while recovery has additional constraints. | The model does not reproduce laboratory neuromuscular measurements. |
| [Baker et al., 2010 — Interaction among skeletal muscle metabolic energy systems during intense exercise](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3005844/) | ATP-resynthesis pathways operate together; relative contribution varies with effort. | ATP availability and pathway widths are normalized teaching variables, not ATP flux assays. |
| [OpenStax — Heart anatomy](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-1-heart-anatomy) | Heart orientation, great vessels and pulmonary/systemic connections. | Original simplified SVG drawing; no textbook artwork copied. |
| [OpenStax — Lungs](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-2-the-lungs) | Asymmetry, cardiac notch, airway branching. | Transparent frontal schematic; not a tomographic or surgical view. |
| [OpenStax — Circulatory pathways](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/20-5-circulatory-pathways) | Branching systemic vessels, paired venous return and pulmonary circuit. | A selected set of major routes, with exaggerated vessel visibility. |
| [OpenStax — Pelvic girdle and lower-limb muscles](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/11-6-appendicular-muscles-of-the-pelvic-girdle-and-lower-limbs) | Anterior quadriceps/adductors and posterior gluteal/hamstring/calf placement. | Surface geometry and fiber lines simplified for legibility. |

No claim that glucose is wholly exhausted, lactate is a poison, ATP availability equals maximal power, or a 20-repetition protocol is suitable for a particular person. Scientific caveats are available on demand so the primary scene remains visual.
