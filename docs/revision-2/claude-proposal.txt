# Revision 2: critique and refinements

The proposal is sound. The narrative (ATP protected, capacity falls while fuel remains, PCr resynthesis depends on oxygen) is correct and well chosen. The main risks are a few understated physiological magnitudes, too many sprint-mode rows that say the same thing, and an anatomical mismatch: the main sprint muscles are mostly on the back of the body, but the view is anterior.

Each point below is tagged **Fact** (supported by literature, but check the figures against the cited papers before encoding them) or **Model** (a design choice).

## Physiology

**1. Widen the ATP floor and demote ATP from a full bar.**
- **Fact:** Biopsy studies of repeated short sprints report net muscle ATP falls of roughly 10–30% by the late reps, with IMP and ammonia accumulating. ATP never empties. A 92–100% band understates this.
- **Model:** Let ATP drift to about 75–85% by late reps. It moves too little to deserve its own bar, so show it as a caption on the energy-source bar instead, e.g. "ATP held ≈ 85%." This keeps the teaching point and frees a row.

**2. Make the stacked energy-source bar show total turnover, not just shares.**
- **Fact:** In 10×6 s / 30 s (Gaitanos), glycolytic ATP production fell several-fold from sprint 1 to sprint 10, while power fell only about a quarter. The aerobic share rose substantially.
- **Model:** A bar normalized to 100% hides two things: the huge difference between sprint and walk, and the rep-to-rep decline. Use these encodings:
  - Bar *length* = total ATP turnover relative to the rep-1 peak.
  - *Segments* = PCr / glycolytic / aerobic shares.
- During walking the bar becomes short and almost entirely aerobic. There is no PCr segment then, because PCr is being rebuilt using aerobic ATP. That visual alone teaches "PCr is a buffer, not free energy."

**3. Merge "stress" and "fatigue" into one row.**
- **Fact:** Sprint fatigue has several causes (PCr availability, Pi and H⁺ accumulation, K⁺ handling, neural drive). No single measurable "stress" quantity exists.
- **Model:** Keep both state variables, but show one **Fatigue** bar in two tones:
  - a fast component that mostly clears within the 30 s walk;
  - a slow component that ratchets up across reps and persists for hours.
- Two separate bars that both rise in sprints and fall in walks would be redundant with each other and with Power.

**4. Combine oxygen and heart rate into one row, and model walking as a cost.**
- **Fact:** VO₂ and HR rise across reps, lag the work bouts, and stay elevated during recovery. Late reps can approach a high fraction of VO₂max.
- **Fact:** Active recovery has been reported to impair PCr resynthesis and repeated-sprint performance compared with passive recovery, because walking competes for oxygen.
- **Model:**
  - Show one row, **Oxygen uptake (% VO₂max)**, with HR as an inline bold number (e.g. "168 bpm"), using an assumed HRmax noted in the parameter file.
  - Add a small walking penalty to the PCr recovery rate. This supports the narrative that "walking doesn't power recovery; oxygen does."

**5. Calibrate to the 10-rep evidence and treat reps 11–20 as extrapolation.**
- **Fact:** The direct anchor is 10×6 s / 30 s. PCr recovery after short sprints has a fast component with a half-time of tens of seconds, so 30 s gives incomplete restoration. PCr does not refill *during* the 6 s sprint.
- **Model:**
  - Fix the check wording to "PCr trough and partial refill within each 36 s cycle."
  - Encode explicit calibration targets as tests: power decrement by rep 10, glycolytic collapse, and a lower pre-sprint PCr in late reps.
  - Make reps 11–20 approach a plateau rather than declining linearly.
  - Note in the "Why?" panel that reps 11–20 are extrapolated.

**6. Fix the day-integration pitfalls.**
- **Facts:**
  - Blood glucose often *rises* during and shortly after high-intensity work (catecholamine-driven hepatic output with suppressed insulin). Do not show a dip.
  - Excess post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC) after sprint intervals is modest. Do not inflate the "afterburn."
  - Glycogen resynthesis is fastest in the first hours after exercise when carbohydrate is available.
  - Depletion in sprints is concentrated in type II fibres.
- **Model:** Define a one-way handoff from the sprint engine to the day model at session end:
  - PCr
  - fast fatigue
  - slow fatigue
  - active-muscle glycogen
  - session energy cost

  PCr normalizes within minutes, slow fatigue over hours, and glycogen only with intake. Keep a single source of truth for glycogen so the two engines can't disagree.

## Anatomy

**7. Get depth and placement right, and fade layers by mode.**
- **Facts:**
  - Kidneys are retroperitoneal, so they sit behind the bowel in an anterior view, and the right kidney is slightly lower.
  - The heart apex points left. The aortic arch curves left, and the inferior vena cava (IVC) runs to the right of the aorta.
  - The right lung has 3 lobes; the left has 2, with a cardiac notch.
  - The liver sits largely under the right ribs.
  - Pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood and pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood. Color vessels by oxygenation, not by artery/vein.
- **Model:** Overlapping semi-transparent layers turn muddy, so use mode masks:
  - Sprint mode: viscera at about 15% opacity; leg muscles, heart, lungs and leg vessels at full opacity.
  - Meal mode: the reverse.

**8. Solve the posterior-muscle problem explicitly.**
- **Fact:** The prime sprint movers (gluteus maximus, hamstrings, gastrocnemius/soleus) are mostly posterior. The anterior view honestly shows quadriceps, adductors, tibialis anterior and a hint of iliopsoas.
- **Model:** In sprint mode only, add a small posterior lower-body inset using the same color scale. Use one muscle encoding, PCr lightness, because it gives a visible rhythm of draining and refilling each rep. Fatigue stays on the right panel; don't encode two variables in muscle color.

## UI

**9. Fix the row order and use exact emphasis rules.**

Sprint-mode rows, top to bottom, never reordered:
1. **Available power** (main row)
2. PCr
3. Muscle glycogen
4. Fatigue (two-tone)
5. O₂ uptake + HR
6. Energy-source bar

Emphasis rules:
- At most two emphasized rows at a time. Emphasized rows get a 2 px accent border and full contrast; all others drop to about 55% opacity.
- **Sprint phase:** Power + Energy sources.
- **Walk phase:** PCr + O₂/HR.
- **From rep 8 onward, during walks:** Fatigue replaces O₂/HR.
- **Power:** solid fill when delivered (sprint), hollow outline when only "available" (walk).
- **Glycogen:** sits next to Power, so "fuel remains, power falls" is visible at a glance.

Units, labelled on each row:
- Power: % of rep-1 peak
- PCr: % of resting
- Glycogen: % of pre-session
- Fatigue: 0–100 index

No pulsing animation. Emphasis transitions are instant when reduced motion is set.

**10. Remove duplicate controls and make time compression honest.**
- Rep counter and countdown appear only in the bottom strip, in large type, not also on the left. The left panel keeps scene name, at most 3 keywords, and "Why?".
- Make strip widths proportional to time: sprints are thin bright slivers (1/6 of each cycle), with warm-up and cooldown segments at the ends.
- Show a speed badge at all times (e.g. "Sprint 1× · Walk 3×"), because a changing clock rate distorts how long things feel.
- Clicking a rep jumps to its start using per-rep state checkpoints. This keeps seeking deterministic without re-simulating from 16:00.
- The full-day view collapses to a thin timeline above the strip.
